# Gradebook: late work, personal dates and bridge columns

Read this for late timing on work being scored in a session (`score.md` sends you here when the pull reports `score-and-late` rows), to fix late marks for a class, to set personal due and lock dates, or to carry several assignments into one gradebook column. Scoring itself is in `score.md`; what the teacher's words mean and when to write are in `AGENTS.md`.

## Lateness in a scoring session

Canvas calculates late point penalties. For work being scored in a session, prepare one batch of first-genuine attempt and accommodation choices with `prepare_late_timing` (CLI: `cse prepare-late-timing FILE`; MCP: `prepare_late_timing` with the packet and student/group choices). Do not re-read or re-time already-scored work in a session. The Engine counts from the applicable deadline to the selected first genuine submission, records school-time provenance, and checks current Canvas state. Use prepared proposals on `score-and-late` grade rows. `late-only` is for an explicit non-session timing task on already-graded work. Never hand-calculate session seconds or substitute a prepared `late-only` row for a grade row. If preparation holds or cannot resolve a timing choice, hold only that timing action and continue the academic assessment when its evidence is sufficient.

1. For each session candidate, use the batch preparation tool against the referenced packet. It confirms the chosen attempt exists in the packet; whether it is the first genuine submission (not blank, placeholder or accidental) is your judgment from the pulled attempts. Later drafts inherit its timing.
2. Read the private `extra_time` fact with the established rules in `context/grading.md`, student accommodations in `context/students.md` and pulled notes, and assignment directions. New pulls include true or false; absence in an older packet is unknown, so refresh that student's evidence when the flag is needed for a timing decision. A documented individual rule takes precedence over a general flag policy; a false flag never cancels a documented accommodation. Neither a bare flag nor a Canvas tag name supplies a duration, multiplier or waiver.
3. Decide the applicable documented allowance (eligibility and how many school days) and whether Canvas's individualized `effective_due_at` already includes it. When the pulled work has `extra_time_school_days`, that is the teacher's saved number for this student from the Students page: use it as the allowance, with a basis such as "Saved extra time: 2 school days", and don't ask again. Without it, fall back to the general allowance in `context/grading.md`. If Canvas has not applied it, send `allowance_school_days` with a private `accommodation_basis` in that student's batch choice and the Engine counts the school days from Canvas's date using `context/school-calendar.md`; do not count days by hand. If the allowance is a documented absolute date, or Canvas's date already includes it, send that date once as `accommodated_deadline` with the basis. Never add the allowance twice and never send both fields. Keep the pulled Canvas date as the `due_at` write guard; the Engine preserves it independently of the counted deadline.
4. The Engine counts from the resolved deadline to the first genuine submission in the calendar's IANA timezone, excluding weekends and listed holidays/non-school ranges while preserving partial-day seconds. Weekdays, including early-release days, count as full calendar dates. The Engine also applies the absences the teacher saved on the Absences page: a day the student was out is not late time, and a deadline that falls on one moves to the student's next day back. An absence before the due date changes nothing. You don't send absences; they come back as `absent_dates`. An on-time result needs no school-time interval. Review the returned timing, private basis, absent dates and skipped dates.
5. For a `score-and-late` candidate, attach the prepared proposal's `late_timing` to its grade row. For `late-only`, use only the prepared attempt and timing fields. The Engine rechecks the attempt, live due date, observed timing and school-calendar calculation before writing lateness. A timing hold does not block an otherwise valid grade or comment.

If `extra_time: true` has no usable documented allowance, hold only that student's timing correction and explain the missing rule. Omit their unresolved choice from preparation and continue valid academic scoring and other students' timing. Ask once for the applicable rule when needed; save a teacher-provided standing flag policy in `context/grading.md`, or an individual accommodation in `context/students.md`, through the existing teacher-context workflow. The Engine does not interpret the flag or invent an allowance. Keep the flag and accommodation basis out of student comments, shared feedback and parent reports.

For a scoring session, prepare the resolved timing candidates for work being scored as one batch before saving results and report any unresolved candidates as held. The typed MCP preparation accepts `packet`, `students`, and `groups` inline; the CLI reads a `late-timing-decisions` file under `courses/`. Inspect proposed, unchanged and held rows. Attach proposals to matching grade rows where `work_purpose` is `score-and-late`. Report corrected or unchanged timing and any held candidates with scoring outcomes. Preparation is read-only; the teacher's go-ahead still comes before any write. See `engine_reference` topic `formats-results` (CLI `help formats-results`) for the complete field shape, and `examples/synthetic-late-timing-decisions` for a synthetic example.

If the due date, first genuine work, calendar coverage or accommodation details are unclear, omit timing for that student or keep the late-only row held, say what's missing, and continue independent work. Extra time changes timing, never the score or feedback. New Quiz comment-only work has no lateness action.

## Fix late marks for a class

When the teacher asks to fix, correct or reconcile late marks for a class (not to score work), run the sweep: `late_sweep` with the course (CLI: `cse late-sweep COURSE [--period NAME]`). It reads every submission Canvas shows as late on ordinary assignments due this grading period and recounts the lateness in school time: weekends and calendar closures don't count, saved absences don't count, and saved extra-time days are added. It doesn't pull or read student work, and it writes nothing to Canvas.

1. Report in plain words, by alias: how many late marks would become on time, how many stay late with fewer days, how many are already right, and each held student with the reason. Say the count of rows marked `check_first_attempt`: those students turned the work in more than once and the sweep timed their earliest attempt without reading it, so the teacher may want to hold them back if the first one was blank. Keep absences and accommodation reasons out of anything students or families see.
2. For a student held because Extra time is ticked with no number, ask the teacher for the number of school days once. It is saved on the Students page beside the Extra time box, or as the general allowance in `context/grading.md`. Then run the sweep again.
3. Wait for the teacher's go-ahead in the conversation. A request that already said to fix and post is that go-ahead.
4. Post each results file the sweep listed (`post_results`; CLI `cse post FILE`). To leave a student out, remove their row from that file first. The Engine rechecks each student's attempt, due date, calendar and absences before writing, and holds a row when any of them changed.
5. Report what was corrected and what was held. Canvas recalculates the late penalty itself.

The sweep leaves out group assignments, New Quizzes, excused work, custom grade statuses and work never turned in. If the school calendar is missing or doesn't cover the dates, the sweep stops and says so: fix `context/school-calendar.md` first.

## Personal dates and reopening

When the course has a late-work limit, the Canvas lock at the cutoff also locks students who have extra time or were absent. `personal_dates` (CLI: `cse personal-dates COURSE [ASSIGNMENT_ID] [--preview]`) gives individual students their own Canvas due and lock dates on one assignment, or on the course's published assignments due in the last 21 days or later:

- a student with saved extra time (days, percent or the general allowance) gets the later due date and a lock that follows it;
- a student absent on the due date gets the next day back;
- only when the teacher has set a correction window (`late_work.correction_school_days`) and asks for it, a student whose work came in on time is reopened for corrections through that window, even past the cutoff. Without a teacher-set window, no one is reopened.

Dates only move later; an existing personal due date is kept; a student in a Canvas date shared with others is held; group assignments are skipped. Run it with `preview` first and report by alias what would be set and why (keep absences and accommodations out of anything students or families see), then wait for the teacher's go-ahead, write, and report written and held rows. There is no scheduler: run it after each due date, and right after creating an assignment in a course with extra-time students. Work already past the cutoff on an assignment with no lock is not zeroed by anything; say so if the teacher asks.

A policy zero from the cutoff is not "not turned in": see `parent-updates.md` and `family-contact.md` for wording.

## Bridge column (one gradebook column from several assignments)

When students took different versions of the same work (each book club's own quiz) and the teacher wants one column, ask once which assignment group the column belongs in, then create an assignment with `submission_types: [none]`, the same points, in that group. Run `carry_grades` with `preview`, show the teacher the counts per source, the held students by alias with the reason, and three sample rows, and write only after their go-ahead. Held students are the teacher's call: never enter a zero or a blank for them. The sources still count until the teacher approves moving them to a no-weight assignment group (an assignment update with `assignment_group_id`); say what that does to course grades first. Publishing the column and posting its grades are the teacher's decisions.
