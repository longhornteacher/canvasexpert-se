# Scoring rules

Teacher policy updated 2026-10-18 (folder names: Dev, UserInstall, UserSync; see [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md)). The agent assesses work honestly using the Canvas rubric when available, or a common-sense local scoring basis when absent; the Engine posts the supported assessment output. A missing Canvas rubric is not a blocker. For ordinary assignments, always post the computed assessment, even below an existing entered grade. Never consider a higher entered grade when choosing the assessment or deciding to post; the teacher handles later raises.

Honest-effort floor (decided 2026-10-24): a course may set `effort_floor` in `context/courses.md` (WebUI Courses page), a percent of the assignment's points. When the agent judges a submission a genuine attempt it marks the grade row `honest_effort: true`; if the computed assessment is below the floor, the Engine enters the floor as the Canvas grade. The assessment, rubric and comment score lines stay as earned, and the comment adds "Entered as N/P (class minimum for an honest attempt)". Blank, placeholder or accidental work, and work that is not a real try, is not marked and gets no floor. Canvas then applies any late deduction to the entered grade. Shared group grades (`kind: group-results` shared mode) do not use the floor; individual group rows do.

New Quizzes, including written-response items, are open for authoring, use and scoring within supported limits. For written work, pull the explicitly selected quiz by assignment ID, assess typed responses and uploaded files, and use `operation: comment-only` with a `comment_only` body containing the exact `attempt`, component criterion scores and `feedback`. Use the attached rubric or an explicit local `scoring_basis`; the Engine validates arithmetic and posts one whole-assignment comment per student. The comment starts with criterion scores and contains no aggregate quiz assessment. With `item_scores` it writes each written item's points (the part's rubric result scaled to the item's points, rounded once to two places) and the part's feedback as the item comment through an undocumented internal New Quizzes call, checked live 2026-10-29; Canvas computes the quiz total. It never writes a rubric assessment or late timing. Without `item_scores`, or for a held student, the teacher enters item points in SpeedGrader. Missing/unreadable evidence holds that student's feedback and independent students continue. Local-test and pending live evidence labels describe confidence, not a blanket prohibition on teacher use.

For feedback without scores, `operation: comment-only` with `comment_only.header: none` and no components needs no rubric, scoring basis or interpretation. Keep exact pulled identity/attempt and discussion-evidence guards, the teacher's go-ahead before posting, duplicate protection and persisted outcomes. Supplied components are always validated; hiding the header never waives their arithmetic. Feedback-only comments do not count as completed New Quiz scoring in session discovery.

Keep the rubric and feedback honest. Do not inflate criteria to match a teacher's final grade or call a skipped rubric complete. Compare revisions with the strongest preceding academic attempt from work and rubric evidence.

Possible AI use or copying (decided 2026-10-28): the agent flags, the teacher decides. Flags rest on the pulled evidence only (leftover chat text, invented evidence, wording matching a classmate or source, writing far above grade level from a student known to work at or below it (on-level section, Silver/Green tier, weaker earlier work), or two weaker independent signals such as a sharp break from the student's earlier work; far-above-grade writing alone is a weak flag), never on an outside detector or web search with student text. A flag does not change the honest score, stays out of every student- and family-facing output, and is reported in the conversation with exact short excerpts and the signal each shows. Flagged students are held when the teacher has already said to post; otherwise the teacher is asked. The judgment lives in `playbooks/score.md`.

For a shared group assignment, `pull-work` produces source-linked `group-packet` and `group-work` evidence while retaining each student's submission, attempt, grade, effective dates, flags and comments. A shared artifact does not prove each member contributed. Treat unknown membership, placeholder/no-attempt rows, incomplete audience reads and unreadable files as unknown evidence rather than missing work. If current artifacts or attempts differ, keep shared assessment on hold. A ready group (known `grade_group_students_individually: false`, complete assignment-specific audience, one current artifact and attempt) is assessed once with `kind: group-results`: the shared grade and rubric are computed with the ordinary exact arithmetic, never multiplied by contribution, and reach every member together, so a subset request, changed member or attempt, or excused or hidden member holds that group. Whole-group comments and private per-student comments are separate actions; private text never enters shared payloads. When Canvas grades each student individually (`grade_group_students_individually: true`), use `operation: individual` rows: each student's grade is computed from that student's observed work and your criteria, posted only to that student, never multiplied by contribution; one student's resubmission holds only that student, and an optional separate shared comment reaches the whole audience. Ordinary `results` remain refused on any group assignment. Pulling evidence alone never authorizes a write.

For a graded discussion (an assignment Canvas links to a discussion topic), `pull-work` verifies the association with Canvas and reads the whole conversation once into a `discussion-packet` beside the student packet. Each student-work file carries `discussion_evidence`: the packet path, coverage status (`complete`, `partial` or `unavailable`), the source-qualified references of that student's own entries, and a digest of exactly those posts. Other participants' posts are context, not the student's evidence. Every grade or comment-only result must copy that digest into its `evidence` (and may list cited `context_refs`). Before posting, the Engine re-reads the conversation: a student whose assessed post or cited context changed, whose evidence was unavailable, or whose coverage was partial while a component is scored `missing`, is held alone; a changed topic/assignment link holds everyone; a checkpointed discussion holds only its grade (scoring happens in SpeedGrader). Judge quality from the whole conversation, never from reply counts, and never turn unknown authors or unread replies into zeros. Comments stay individual: no `group_comment` is sent.

The agent infers response units and defensible weights. For a rubric repeated across components, normalize component weights to shares a_i; aggregate each criterion q_j = sum_i(a_i * s_ij). Ordinary assignment grades assess P * sum_j(q_j) / sum_j(m_j), using live assignment maximum P and rubric criterion maxima m_j. New Quiz comment-only results use validated component/criterion arithmetic without displaying or posting an aggregate quiz assessment. If criteria already correspond to distinct prompts, use that mapping instead of repeating them. Preserve calculation precision; round proposed posting/display scores to two decimals with deliberate precision. Use real rating IDs only when they truthfully match the awarded points. Zero-point descriptive criteria do not enter the denominator; do not invent a numeric basis for a wholly unscored rubric. Work without a Canvas rubric requires an explicit local scoring basis.

Ordinary assignment feedback order: overall computed assessment, compact per-response criterion scores, conversational glows/grows grounded in the work. New Quiz written-work feedback starts with criterion scores, then glows/grows; omit an aggregate quiz assessment. Full rubric definitions remain in Canvas. Teacher-only notes never enter student output.

Rubrics the Engine attaches disable automatic grade calculation. Do not silently change existing rubric coupling. For ordinary assignments, post the honest rubric assessment and computed grade normally, including coupled assignments. For New Quiz written work, the rubric is a scoring basis for the comment only. An accepted grade/rubric/comment write counts as done: report once and stop, without post-write readbacks or reply-shape confirmation. Preserve pre-write identity, attempt, scope, basis and duplicate checks; resume genuinely failed or interrupted work from saved outcomes. Independent-rubric and no-rubric work need no workspace certification metadata.

Canvas alone calculates late point penalties. Broad scoring sessions find unscored submissions Canvas reports as needing grading, including work not yet due. They report closed-period assignments without pulling submissions; CE-SE does not apply a grading-period filter. A bare request for a scoring session ends with the assignment list and a question about what to score. Apply an already narrowed request using common sense from the report's assignment group and due-state columns. Resubmissions after grading are ordinary unscored work and are assessed normally. A succeeded New Quiz comment for the current attempt does not need to be pulled again while Canvas still awaits written-item points; report the quiz and student count as “comment posted, item points not entered” and remind the teacher to enter those points in SpeedGrader.

Prepare timing only for work the session is scoring, using batch `prepare_late_timing`. The agent identifies the first genuine submission (not blank, placeholder or accidental; later drafts inherit its timing) and any established accommodation. The Engine reads `context/school-calendar.md` to count school time from the applicable deadline to the selected submission, including non-school dates and ranges; an accommodated deadline changes the calculation, while `due_at` remains Canvas's date checked before write. Prepared proposals attach to `score-and-late` grade rows. Do not re-read or re-time already-scored work in a session; there is no session option for that. `late-only` remains available only for an explicit non-session timing task and changes only Canvas's late-policy status and seconds-late override. It never changes the grade or points. Unknown or changed evidence holds that timing action and leaves independent academic work available. New Quiz comment-only results carry no lateness actions. See [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md) for current boundaries.

Parent reports use the active grading period in `context/school-calendar.md` and the calendar's IANA timezone. The saved report records the scope and includes every published, student-visible, required assignment due from the later of period start or the configured correction window through `as_of`; an explicit full-period pull instead starts at period start; upcoming work is shown separately for at most 7 calendar days and clipped at period end. Individual due dates take precedence over course dates, including an explicit undated override. Missing/0, Corrections and History must remain subsets of this inventory. For the default layout, a complete, empty work window is a skip regardless of current course grade or Due Soon rows. Default email and print include every inventory row, even when the agent chooses no rows for optional draft wording. The explicitly requested weekly ELA email layout instead shows its computed Missing and Failing lists, including an empty state when neither has items. Do not disclose unposted grades; `posted_at: null` keeps work in the inventory but withholds grade values and feedback.

Posting gate (decided 2026-10-10; changed 2026-10-24 after real scoring sessions): the agent reports the scored work in the conversation (score ranges, general impressions, held students and timing) and posts on the teacher's go-ahead there, or directly when the request already included posting. Scoring and authoring review-page commands were retired on 2026-10-28; the exact feedback example comes from `validate RESULTS --show STUDENT_REF` (MCP `validate_document.show`). Private family review is unchanged. Curve suggestions remain hypothetical: no curve is applied without a separate requested feature, and effort floors never silently become New Quiz item points or fudge points. The check-in changes when posting happens, never what is posted: changes the teacher asks for are made in the results file and validated, and a held student's row is left out of the post.
